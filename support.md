## Common questions

**What is the success rate?**
The percentage of historical N-year market windows (since 1871) in which your portfolio survived without depleting. A 95% success rate means your plan would have lasted in 95 of every 100 historical scenarios.

**Where does the data come from?**
Real historical US market data: large-cap returns, long-term Treasury returns, T-bill yields, CPI inflation, and (for Pro asset classes) small-cap, corporate bonds, real estate, gold, and TIPS. Sources are listed in Settings → Data sources, with full attribution to Robert Shiller (Yale), Aswath Damodaran (NYU Stern), FRED, and NBER.

**Is fuegoCalc financial advice?**
No. fuegoCalc is an educational tool that simulates how your plan would have fared in past market scenarios. It is not predictive of future results, and it is not a substitute for advice from a qualified financial planner.

**My results changed after switching the inflation assumption — why?**
The simulation runs in real (today's) dollars. When you switch from "Historical CPI" to a fixed rate, every year of every historical window uses your fixed rate instead of the actual CPI of that period. Real returns and the deflation of nominal income streams (like non-COLA Social Security) all adjust accordingly.

**TIPS allocation broke my Results — why?**
TIPS data starts in 1982, so allocating to TIPS restricts the historical windows the simulation can use. With a 50-year plan you'd run out of windows. The Results tab will tell you the longest plan that fits and which class is the limiting factor.

**Why is my portfolio negative in the chart?**
The simulation continues running after a portfolio depletes, showing the cumulative shortfall. This isn't realistic borrowing — it's an "underwater" indicator showing how deep a failed plan would be. Plans that succeeded never go negative.

## Reporting bugs

Email **fuegocalc-dev@prodigalpanda.com** with:

1. What you were doing
2. What you expected to happen
3. What actually happened
4. Your iPhone model and iOS version

A screenshot helps a lot.
